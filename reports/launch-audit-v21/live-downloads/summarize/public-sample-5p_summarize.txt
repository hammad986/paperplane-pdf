**Executive Summary** 
This paper proposes a conceptual framework for making Open Science a first-class requirement in Software Engineering (SE), arguing that current cultural and technical barriers prevent widespread adoption. The authors identify five interconnected building blocks—recognition and reward systems, artifact valuation, infrastructure-as-code for research software, Open Science education and training by design, and Open Science tooling—and critique the existing Artifact Evaluation Track badging system for failing to make artifact sharing the default expectation. The framework envisions systemic shifts in SE careers, education, and tooling to align research practice with Open Science principles, ultimately aiming for greater scientific and social impact.

**Key Details, Decisions, Numbers, and Deadlines** 
- The framework treats Open Science as a first-class requirement for SE research recognition and reward systems, moving beyond optional practices [Page 1]. 
- Research artifacts must be considered integral parts of publications, on par with papers themselves [Page 2]. 
- Research software should be accompanied by infrastructure-as-code to support Empirical Software Engineering and facilitate artifact evaluation during review [Page 2]. 
- Open Science education and training are essential so the community adopts principles by design; this includes developing openness definitions, open methodologies, and courses based on Open Educational Resources [Page 4]. 
- “Open Science by Design” envisions openness throughout the entire research life cycle, adhering to FAIR principles, and advocates for early integration into formal SE education, including graduate and undergraduate theses [Page 4]. 
- An introductory course, “Open Science 101,” is proposed for SE curricula to cover core concepts and fundamentals of open research [Page 4]. 
- Artifact Evaluation Tracks currently use badges to indicate artifact availability/quality, but this does not make sharing the default outcome, conflicting with Open Science ideals. The paper recommends either incorporating evaluation into the peer-review process (where resources allow) or reserving it for advanced recognition (e.g., ACM “Results Validated” badges) [Page 3]. 
- The vision was presented at FSE Companion ’24, July 15–19, 2024, in Porto de Galinhas, Brazil [Page 2].
